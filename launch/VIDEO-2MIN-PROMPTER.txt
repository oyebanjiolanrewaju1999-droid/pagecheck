Your website might be losing customers right now,
and you would never know it.
Not because it's ugly —
because of things you can't see.

When someone finds your business on Google,
four things decide whether they click:
your title,
the description under it,
the image that shows when someone shares your link,
and how fast the page loads.

Get those wrong and you don't get an error.
You just get fewer customers,
and no way to know why.

So I built a free tool called PageCheck.
You paste your website address,
and in about eight seconds
it runs thirty checks
and gives you a score out of a hundred.

No sign-up.
No account.
It doesn't store your link.

Let me show you.
I'll use my own site.

Paste the address…
hit audit…
and there it is:
eighty-two out of a hundred, grade B.

Underneath, it tells me the three things to fix first.

My page has no meta description,
so Google is writing its own line under my link.

The share image is missing,
so my links look broken when someone sends them.

And there's no strict transport security header.

That's the part I care about.
It doesn't just tell you something is wrong —
it tells you what to change, in plain English,
and which three to do first.

A business owner can read this in two minutes
and hand it to their web person.
No jargon, no sales call.

It's free, it takes about eight seconds,
and it works in English, French, Spanish and Hindi.

The link is in my bio.
Run your site through it —
and if the score surprises you,
tell me in the comments.
